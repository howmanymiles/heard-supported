## PIP

### `rj-gmcl-cs-safely`: RJ, GMcL and CS v SSWP (PIP) [2017] UKUT 105 (AAC); [2017] AACR 32
*UT (AAC) three-judge panel · verified from primary source · topics: pip:reg4-2A, pip:reg4-2A-safely, pip:supervision, pip:reg7*

An activity is not carried out 'safely' (reg 4(2A) and 4(4)(a)) where there is a real possibility of harm that cannot be ignored; harm need not be more likely than not, and both the likelihood and the severity of the potential harm are relevant. 'Safety' in the Schedule 1 definition of supervision is read the same way, and if the claimant cannot do the activity safely or needs supervision on the majority of days the descriptor can apply even if the harmful event itself occurs on fewer days.

[Decision](https://www.gov.uk/administrative-appeals-tribunal-decisions/rj-gmcl-and-cs-v-secretary-of-state-for-work-and-pensions-v-rj-pip-2017-ukut-105-aac)  
**Check:** Panel: Lady Carmichael, UTJ Knowles QC, UTJ Markus QC; decided 9 March 2017. Key passage reported at [56]. Binding on FtT in preference to earlier single-judge decisions that focused on infrequency/remoteness of harm. Check the exact wording of the headnote point on the 50%-of-days interaction with reg 7 against the full text.

### `sswp-v-mm-uksc-social-support`: Secretary of State for Work and Pensions v MM [2019] UKSC 34; [2019] AACR 26
*Supreme Court · verified from primary source · topics: pip:activity9, pip:prompting*

'Social support' in daily living descriptor 9c is not limited to physical assistance and can include prompting; what distinguishes 9c from 9b is that the claimant needs the support of a person trained or experienced in assisting people to engage in social situations. The support need not be given at the same time as, or be present during, the engagement, so support given in advance or afterwards can qualify; whether 9c applies is a question of fact and degree.

[Decision](https://caselaw.nationalarchives.gov.uk/uksc/2019/34)  
**Check:** Scottish appeal from the Inner House; reported in AACR as SSWP v MMcK. Lady Black gave the lead judgment (unanimous), 18 July 2019. AACR report confirmed at https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/6239cbae8fa8f540e78ab43a/_2019__AACR_26ws.pdf. The Court declined to adopt the Inner House's 'temporal or causal link' and 'increased effectiveness' formulations. Familiarity alone (e.g. a close relative) is not enough — the trained/experienced quality must be what the claimant relies on.

### `mh-overwhelming-psychological-distress`: MH v SSWP (PIP) [2016] UKUT 531 (AAC); [2018] AACR 12
*UT (AAC) three-judge panel · verified from primary source · topics: pip:activity11, pip:activity9, pip:prompting*

In mobility activity 1, 'overwhelming psychological distress' is not confined to descriptors 1b and 1e; descriptors 1d and 1f must be read in the light of 1b and 1e, and following a route includes but is not limited to navigation. Anxiety counts in this way only where it amounts to overwhelming psychological distress, a high threshold, and an award for prompting under daily living activity 9 does not automatically mean entitlement under mobility activity 1 (or vice versa), though a tribunal must not ignore a relevant finding made under one when considering the other.

[Decision](https://www.gov.uk/administrative-appeals-tribunal-decisions/mh-v-secretary-of-state-for-work-and-pensions-pip-2016-ukut-0531-aac)  
**Check:** Panel: Judges Rowland, Rowley and Hemingway; 28 November 2016. Three conjoined appeals. Also holds that inability to communicate with people to find the way when lost is irrelevant to following a route, and that physical symptoms of psychological distress affecting only outdoor journeys are not counted under mobility activity 2 where 1e applies. The Government amended mobility activity 1 by regulations in 2017 in response to related case law, and those amendments were later challenged in the High Court — expert should check the current statutory wording and that litigation (not verified here).

### `lc-reg7-dressing-cooking`: LC v SSWP (PIP) [2016] UKUT 150 (AAC)

### `eg-reg7-more-than-trivial`: EG v SSWP (PIP) [2017] UKUT 101 (AAC)
*UT (AAC) · verified from primary source · topics: pip:reg7, pip:activity9*

Applying TR v SSWP (PIP) [2016] UKUT 626 (AAC), a day counts towards the reg 7 majority-of-days requirement if on that day the claimant's ability to carry out the activity is affected to more than a trivial extent. Ability must be assessed in normal daily life rather than a 'sheltered' version of it, so avoidance strategies forced by the disability (as opposed to genuine choice) should not be used to reduce the count.

[Decision](https://caselaw.nationalarchives.gov.uk/ukut/aac/2017/101)  
**Check:** Judge Paula Gray, CPIP/2306/2015, signed 6 March 2017; key passages at [47]-[50]. Relies on TR v SSWP (PIP) [2016] UKUT 626 (AAC) at [31]-[34] — TR itself was cited in EG but not independently verified here. Remitted, so no final day-count.

### `cw-aids-normal-use`: CW v SSWP (PIP) [2016] UKUT 197 (AAC); [2016] AACR 44
*UT (AAC) · verified from primary source · topics: pip:aids, pip:activity6*

An aid need not be designed, made or sold for the purpose; any item used as an aid can qualify. But it must help overcome an impaired function required for that aspect of the activity, and on the facts needing to sit on a bed or chair to dress reflected one normal manner of dressing rather than an impaired function, so it was not an aid.

[Decision](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/58862281e5274a7a68000014/_2016__AACR_44ws.pdf)  
**Check:** Judge Jacobs, CPIP/3369/2015, 20 April 2016. Contrast NA v SSWP [2015] UKUT 572 (AAC). Qualified by BC v SSWP [2024] UKUT 450 (AAC), which holds CW does not establish that 'normal behaviour' is decisive. Relevant to perching stool / chair arguments in activities 1, 4 and 6.

### `bc-aids-functional-role`: BC v SSWP [2024] UKUT 450 (AAC)
*UT (AAC) · verified from primary source · topics: pip:aids, pip:activity4*

CW and AP do not establish that ordinary or 'normal' use by people without impairment is decisive of whether a device is an aid; the focus is on the device's functional role for the claimant. Bath handles can be an aid where the claimant could not get into or out of the bath without them, even though they are part of the bath and used by others.

[Decision](https://www.gov.uk/administrative-appeals-tribunal-decisions/bc-v-secretary-of-state-for-work-and-pensions-2024-ukut-450-aac)  
**Check:** Judge Brewer, UA-2024-000064-PIP, decided 13 November 2024 (published November 2025). Recent single-judge decision qualifying CW [2016] UKUT 197 (AAC) and AP [2016] UKUT 501 (AAC); check for any later decisions disagreeing.

### `ps-acceptable-standard-pain`: PS v SSWP (PIP) [2016] UKUT 326 (AAC)
*UT (AAC) · verified from primary source · topics: pip:reg4-2A, pip:activity12*

Pain is potentially relevant to whether a claimant can move around 'to an acceptable standard' under reg 4(2A), and a tribunal must consider any limb of reg 4(2A) put in issue by the evidence even if not expressly raised; the tribunal erred by not considering whether the claimant walked the distance to an acceptable standard given his pain.

[Decision](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/57971f5040f0b652dd00017c/CPIP_0665_2016-00.pdf)  
**Check:** Judge Markus QC, CPIP/665/2016, 11 July 2016. 'Acceptable standard' is not defined in the Regulations. The judge noted that a stoical claimant who pushes through severe pain may still fail the acceptable standard test, though such cases should be rare. Reg 4(2A) does not arise where the person cannot do the activity at all. Tag pip:activity12 = mobility activity 2 (moving around).

### `ls-reasonable-time-repeatedly`: LS v SSWP (PIP) [2016] UKUT 573 (AAC)
*UT (AAC) · verified from primary source · topics: pip:reg4-2A-reasonable-time, pip:reg4-2A-repeatedly, pip:activity12, reasons*

## UC WCA (including ESA decisions applied to UC)

### `charlton-substantial-risk`: Charlton v SSWP [2009] EWCA Civ 42
*Court of Appeal · verified from primary source · topics: wca:substantial-risk-lcw*

The 'substantial risk' exception applies only after the claimant fails the functional assessment, and the risk must arise from being found capable of work — assessed in the context of the journey to or from work or in the workplace. The decision-maker must consider the range or type of work the claimant could do (not specific jobs) and ask whether there is an adequate range of work the claimant could do without substantial risk to their own or anyone else's health; the inquiry is inquisitorial with no formal burden of proof.

[Decision](https://caselaw.nationalarchives.gov.uk/ewca/civ/2009/42)  
**Check:** Moses LJ (Pill and Lloyd LJJ agreeing), 6 February 2009; reported as R(IB) 2/09. Decided on incapacity benefit (Social Security (Incapacity for Work) (General) Regulations 1995, reg 27(b)); applied by the UT to ESA reg 29 and to UC Sch 8 para 4 (see ET v SSWP (UC) [2021] UKUT 47 (AAC) and AH [2013] UKUT 118 (AAC) for LCWRA). The Court noted a rare exception where the finding of capability itself could create the risk (e.g. deterioration in anxiety/depression).

### `im-2014-wra-substantial-risk`: IM v SSWP (ESA) [2014] UKUT 412 (AAC); [2015] AACR 10
*UT (AAC) three-judge panel · verified from primary source · topics: wca:substantial-risk-lcwra*

Under ESA reg 35(2), 'substantial risk' means a risk that cannot sensibly be ignored given the nature and gravity of the feared harm. It is not enough for the Secretary of State to identify some work-related activity the claimant could do safely: the types of activity the claimant might be required to undertake must be identified, and the Secretary of State should provide the tribunal with information about the work-related activity available in the claimant's area; inability to do an activity, or the risk of sanctions, does not of itself establish substantial risk.

[Decision](https://administrativeappeals.decisions.tribunals.gov.uk/Aspx/view.aspx?id=4295)  
**Check:** CE/3453/2013, 15 September 2014. ESA decision. The UC equivalent is Sch 9 para 4, whose wording refers to risk 'if the claimant were found not to have limited capability for work and work-related activity' (CPAG notes the different wording) — expert to confirm how the UT applies IM to UC. CPAG reports CT v SSWP (ESA) [2021] UKUT 131 (AAC) reminding that IM binds the FtT, and MD v SSWP (UC) [2020] UKUT 215 (AAC) and KS v SSWP (UC) [2021] UKUT 132 (AAC) criticising inaccurate WRA lists in UC cases (not independently verified here).

### `ah-2013-lcwra-charlton`: AH v SSWP (ESA) [2013] UKUT 118 (AAC); [2013] AACR 32
*UT (AAC) · verified from primary source · topics: wca:substantial-risk-lcwra, wca:mobilising*

For ESA reg 35(2) the decision-maker must consider the range of work-related activity the claimant could reasonably undertake, which requires evidence about both the activity and the claimant's health. Terms such as 'repeatedly' and 'reasonable timescale' in the mobilising activity have their ordinary meaning and mean the same in Schedule 3 as in Schedule 2, and tribunals must make findings specific enough to show whether a descriptor applies.

[Decision](https://administrativeappeals.decisions.tribunals.gov.uk/Aspx/view.aspx?id=3744)  
**Check:** Judge Jacobs, CE/1750/2012, 5 March 2013. Companion to ML v SSWP (ESA) [2013] UKUT 174 (AAC); [2013] AACR 33. CPAG describes AH as applying the Charlton approach to LCWRA. Largely superseded on the WRA-evidence point by the three-judge panel in IM [2014] UKUT 412 (AAC). ESA decision; UC equivalent is Sch 9 para 4 and Sch 7.

### `gz-mobilising-findings-repeatedly`: GZ v SSWP (ESA) [2017] UKUT 447 (AAC)
*UT (AAC) · verified from primary source · topics: wca:mobilising, wca:majority-of-time, reasons*

Because the mobilising descriptors overlap, a tribunal must make specific findings on the distance the claimant can mobilise without stopping and must expressly consider the 'repeatedly … within a reasonable timescale' limbs; a claimant able to mobilise between 100 and 200 metres without stopping may still be unable to mobilise 100 metres repeatedly within a reasonable timescale. A general reliance on the 'majority of the time' rule is not a substitute for specific findings.

[Decision](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/5a251b0ced915d458b922ee5/CE_1492_2017-00.pdf)

Majority-of-the-time rule: ESA reg 34(2); for UC LCWRA it is UC Regs 2013 reg 40(4) (confirmed on legislation.gov.uk). ### `ab-getting-about-taxi`: AB v SSWP (ESA) [2016] UKUT 96 (AAC)
