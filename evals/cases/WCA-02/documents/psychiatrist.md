**Background**

She was diagnosed with bipolar affective disorder at 24. She has had two previous hypomanic episodes (2015 and 2019), the second requiring a short admission, and depressive episodes in 2017 and 2024. She was stable on quetiapine from 2019 and worked full time as a team leader in a contact centre. The 2024 depressive episode occurred during a period of performance management at work, while she was adherent to medication, and was managed in the community.

**Presentation leading to admission**

Over approximately two months before admission her mood declined. She stopped eating regular meals and lost around 7 kg. She stopped washing, stopped opening post and did not leave her flat for several weeks. Rent arrears accumulated. Her sister raised concerns with the GP. On admission she was withdrawn, slowed and dehydrated. She described feeling hopeless. There were no psychotic symptoms. She did not report any intent to harm herself.

During the first three weeks of admission nursing staff needed to prompt her to eat and to attend to personal care. This improved over the second half of the admission.

**Current mental state (29 September 2026)**

Casually dressed, adequately kempt; sister reports she showered that morning because of the appointment. Psychomotor slowing less marked than on admission. Mood subjectively "flat", objectively low. Reactive to some degree. Sleep 10 to 12 hours. Appetite poor; eating one meal a day, usually one her sister has prepared. Weight stable since discharge. No psychotic features. No current thoughts of harming herself. Insight good. Concentration poor.

She reports that she does not answer the door to people she does not know and has found visits from unfamiliar home treatment staff distressing. She attends appointments only when accompanied by her sister.

**Risk**

The principal risks are of self-neglect (poor nutrition and personal care, inability to manage her tenancy and finances) and of relapse into a further severe depressive episode. The risk to herself is currently assessed as moderate and is mitigated by daily home treatment team contact and the support of her sister. There is no identified risk to others.

**Opinion on fitness for work and work-related activity (at patient's request)**

She is not fit for work. In my opinion she is also not well enough at present to engage in work-related activity. She is in the early phase of recovery from a severe depressive episode and her mood stabiliser has not yet reached a therapeutic level. Her two most recent depressive episodes both followed periods of sustained external pressure and fear of losing income (performance management in 2024; work search requirements and a warning about a sanction in 2026). I consider that a requirement to attend regular appointments or undertake work preparation activity, with the possibility of a sanction, would carry a substantial risk of precipitating relapse, with a consequent risk to her health through self-neglect.

I would expect this position to be reviewed in around six months. Recovery from episodes of this severity commonly takes several months, and it is not possible to say at this stage how far the lithium will reduce her risk of further episodes.
