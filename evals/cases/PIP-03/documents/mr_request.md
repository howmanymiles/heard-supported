## Request for mandatory reconsideration: PIP decision dated 22 July 2026

We act for the claimant, who has signed an authority for us to do so (enclosed). Please reconsider the decision of 22 July 2026 that she is not entitled to Personal Independence Payment. This request is made within one month of the decision.

We accept the descriptors chosen for activities 2, 3, 4, 7, 8, 9, 10 and 11. We dispute activities 1, 5, 6 and 12. On the correct descriptors she scores 8 points for daily living (standard rate) and 12 points for mobility (enhanced rate).

## Activity 12: Moving around. Descriptor sought: 12e (12 points)

The PA4 (page 4) chose 12b on the basis of an observed walk of 20 to 30 metres, her own estimate of 50 metres, and her walk to the shop. None of these shows that she can stand and move more than 50 metres, still less that she can do so reliably.

1. **The only measured evidence.** Physio letter, page 1: a six-minute walk test on a flat clinic corridor, with her stick, at her own pace. "Walk abandoned at 18 metres when patient stopped due to breathlessness and right knee pain." Her oxygen saturation fell from 93% to 86%, with Borg breathlessness 7/10. After a seated rest of about 4 minutes "She then walked a further 12 metres before stopping again and declined to continue." The physiotherapist records that the patient described it as an average day (page 1), and that her distance "was considerably less than her own estimate" (page 2).

--- Page 2 ---

2. **"A single occasion."** The PA4 sets the test aside as a single occasion. It is the only measured walk on file, carried out by a treating clinician on a day she described as average. It is consistent with the rest of the medical evidence: MRC dyspnoea scale 4 and "Breathless on minimal exertion" (GP letter, pages 1 and 2), and the OT's observation that after the 7-metre walk from her living room to her bathroom she had to sit for 2 to 3 minutes to recover her breath before she could continue (OT report, page 3).
3. **The observed walk.** Taken at face value, walking 20 to 30 metres once, slowly, with a stick, and arriving breathless (PA4, page 2) is not evidence of walking more than 50 metres. It says nothing about whether she could then go on, or do it again.
4. **Reliability.** Under regulation 4(2A) she can only be treated as able to move a given distance if she can do so safely, to an acceptable standard, repeatedly and within a reasonable time. On the test she needed 4 minutes' seated rest for her saturation to recover, then managed only 12 metres more. She cannot reliably move more than 20 metres.
5. **Her estimate and the shop.** Her "about 50 metres" (PIP2, page 3) is her own estimate, which the physiotherapist tested and found to be wrong. The shop is at the end of her street. She tells us she has to stop more than once on the way to get her breath, and does not go at all when her chest is bad. "Most days" is her word on the PIP2; it is a description of how often she goes out, not of how far she walks before stopping.

The medical evidence describes her ordinary level of function, not a bad day, so 12e applies on the majority of days (regulation 7). We are asking her to keep a diary of her walking and will send it when we have it.

## Activity 1: Preparing food. Descriptor sought: 1b (2 points)

The PA4 (page 3) accepts that she sits to prepare vegetables but calls this a preference. Her PIP2 (page 1) says why she sits: "I use the microwave a lot because I can't stand at the cooker for long without getting puffed out. If I'm doing potatoes I sit down to peel them." The stool is used because of her breathlessness and knee pain, not for comfort. The OT offered her a perching stool for the kitchen (OT report, page 4), which shows the clinical view that she needs to sit to prepare food. She declined it only because she already uses her own stool for the same purpose. A stool used for this reason is an aid. In the alternative, 1c applies, as she relies on the microwave because she cannot stand at the cooker. Either way the score is 2 points.

## Activity 5: Managing toilet needs. Descriptor sought: 5b (2 points)

The OT observed in her home that she "Rises from WC with difficulty due to knee pain" (OT report, page 3), and fitted a toilet frame: "Patient tried it and found rising much easier" (page 4). The PA4 (page 3) sets this aside because she rose once from a chair at the assessment centre. That chair had arms, and she used both of them. A standard-height WC has no arms, which is why the frame was fitted. The physiotherapist also recorded that she needed both arms and two attempts to stand from a standard chair (physio letter, page 1). Her PIP2 answer that she has "No problems with the toilet itself" (page 2) was written on 30 June 2026, four weeks after the frame was fitted, so it describes managing with the frame, not without it. Our adviser saw the frame in place around her WC when visiting her at home on 14 July 2026.

## Activity 6: Dressing and undressing. Descriptor sought: 6b (2 points)

The PA4 history (page 1) records that the OT issued a long-handled shoe horn, but the activity 6 justification (page 3) does not mention it. It relies on her reaching mid-shin when seated, which is not the same as reaching her feet. The OT found she is "unable to flex knees enough to reach feet comfortably" (OT report, page 3) and that she "reports using it daily to put on her shoes" (page 4). Putting on shoes is part of dressing.
