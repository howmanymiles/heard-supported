## Section 4: Reasons for appealing

The decision of 22 July 2026, as revised on 9 September 2026, awards 4 points for daily living (4b, 6b) and 4 points for mobility (12b). We accept the revision of activity 6 to 6b and do not dispute activities 2, 3, 4, 7, 8, 9, 10 and 11. We say the appellant scores 8 points for daily living (standard rate) and 12 points for mobility (enhanced rate).

**Activity 12, moving around: 12e sought (12 points).** The only measured walk on file stopped at 18 metres, with her oxygen saturation falling to 86%; after a 4-minute rest she walked only 12 metres more (physiotherapy letter 19 May 2026, page 1). The physiotherapist recorded it as an average day. The decision maker relies instead on one observed walk of 20 to 30 metres, which does not show she can move more than 50 metres, and does not consider whether she can walk any distance over 20 metres repeatedly and within a reasonable time (regulation 4(2A)). Her own estimate of 50 metres was disproved by the test. She stops more than once on the way to the shop at the end of her street.

**Activity 1, preparing food: 1b sought (2 points), or 1c in the alternative.** She sits to prepare food because she cannot stand long enough (PIP2, page 1). The occupational therapist offered a perching stool for the kitchen, confirming a clinical need to sit. Her own stool does the same job. It is an aid, not a preference.

**Activity 5, managing toilet needs: 5b sought (2 points).** The occupational therapist saw her rise from the WC with difficulty and fitted a toilet frame (OT report 2 June 2026, pages 3 and 4). The decision relies on her rising once from a chair with arms at the assessment centre. Her PIP2 answer was given after the frame was fitted.

Further evidence: a walking diary and a written submission will follow before the hearing.
