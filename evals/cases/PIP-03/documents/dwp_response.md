## Section 1: Summary of the decision under appeal

The decision maker decided that the appellant is not entitled to either component of PIP from and including 28 May 2026. On mandatory reconsideration the decision maker scored 4 points for daily living activities and 4 points for mobility activities. A score of at least 8 points is needed for the standard rate of either component.

The appellant disputes activities 1 (preparing food), 5 (managing toilet needs) and 12 (moving around). She asks for 8 points for daily living and 12 points for mobility.

## Section 4: The law

Welfare Reform Act 2012, sections 78 and 79: entitlement to the daily living and mobility components depends on whether the claimant's ability to carry out daily living activities or mobility activities is limited, or severely limited, by their physical or mental condition.

Social Security (Personal Independence Payment) Regulations 2013:

- **Regulation 4** (assessment of ability). Ability is assessed on the basis of the claimant's ability "whilst wearing or using any aid or appliance which C normally wears or uses", or as if using an aid or appliance they could reasonably be expected to use. Regulation 4(2A) provides that a claimant is to be assessed as satisfying a descriptor only if they can do so "safely; to an acceptable standard; repeatedly; and within a reasonable time period".
- **Regulation 5** (daily living component). A claimant has limited ability to carry out daily living activities if they obtain at least 8 points, and severely limited ability with at least 12 points.
- **Regulation 6** (mobility component). The same thresholds apply to the mobility activities.
- **Regulation 7** (satisfying a descriptor). A descriptor applies where it is satisfied "on over 50% of the days of the required period".

The descriptors are set out in Schedule 1. "Aid or appliance" is defined in Schedule 1, Part 1 as a device which improves, provides or replaces impaired physical or mental function.

The decision maker must consider all the evidence and decide what weight to give it. The health professional's report is advice to the decision maker and is not binding.

## Section 5: The appellant's circumstances

The appellant is a woman in her early sixties who lives alone in a ground-floor flat. She has chronic obstructive pulmonary disease (COPD), osteoarthritis of both knees (right worse than left) and hypertension. The GP records spirometry in 2023 with FEV1 42% predicted, and a hospital admission for an infective exacerbation in November 2025 with a further exacerbation managed at home in March 2026. She uses inhalers via a spacer, amlodipine, paracetamol and ibuprofen gel, and holds a rescue pack at home. She uses a stick outdoors. She has been placed on the waiting list for pulmonary rehabilitation.

At the consultation on 16 July 2026 she described a typical day in which she gets herself washed and dressed, makes tea and toast, prepares soup or a microwave meal at lunchtime and a microwave meal in the evening, and walks to the corner shop most days for milk and a paper. She showers twice a week using a shower seat and manages her own bills and shopping.

## Section 6: The decision maker's reasons on the disputed activities

### Activity 12: Moving around (descriptor b, 4 points)

The appellant asks for descriptor e (12 points), on the basis that she cannot stand and then move more than 20 metres.

The decision maker has considered the physiotherapy report of 19 May 2026, which records that a six-minute walk test was stopped at 18 metres. The decision maker notes that the appellant described that day as an average day. However, the walk test is a single snapshot of the appellant's walking on one day, in a clinic setting, and does not outweigh the other evidence of her walking.

On 16 July 2026 the health professional observed the appellant walk from the waiting room to the assessment room, a distance of approximately 20-30 metres, with a stick, without stopping. This is a direct observation by a trained health professional and is considered the most reliable evidence of her walking ability. It shows she was able to walk further than the 18 metres recorded in May without needing to stop.

In her PIP2 the appellant said: "I can walk about 50 metres before I need to stop and get my breath." She also said she goes to the corner shop most days. The decision maker accepts that the appellant becomes breathless and that her knees ache, but the appellant's own account, made in her own words, is that she is able to walk to the shop at the end of her street on most days. This is consistent with an ability to walk more than 50 metres.

The representative argues that the appellant stops more than once on the way to the shop. No distance to the shop has been provided, and the GP states that walking distance has not been formally assessed. The decision maker is satisfied that the appellant can walk more than 50 metres safely and within a reasonable time, using her stick, at a slow pace.

Taking all of the evidence into account, the decision maker is satisfied that the appellant can stand and then move more than 50 metres but no more than 200 metres, either aided or unaided, on the majority of days. Descriptor 12b applies.

### Activity 1: Preparing food (descriptor a, 0 points)

The appellant asks for descriptor b (needs to use an aid or appliance), or descriptor c (can cook a simple meal only using a microwave).

The appellant states that she manages most of the time, that she uses the microwave a lot and that she sits to peel potatoes. She prepares a simple meal twice each day. The occupational therapist offered the appellant a perching stool for the kitchen and she declined it. The decision maker considers that her use of her own kitchen stool is a matter of personal preference rather than a need arising from her condition, and that she is able to stand for long enough to prepare and cook a simple meal. Grip and upper limb function were normal on examination.

A simple meal may be cooked using a microwave. The appellant's preference for microwave meals does not of itself show that she cannot cook a simple meal using a conventional cooker. There is no evidence that she has been advised not to use a cooker. Descriptor 1a applies.

### Activity 5: Managing toilet needs or incontinence (descriptor a, 0 points)

The appellant asks for descriptor b (needs to use an aid or appliance).

The occupational therapy report of 2 June 2026 records that the appellant rises from the WC with difficulty due to knee pain and that a toilet frame was fitted on that visit. The decision maker notes that the toilet frame was fitted on the day of the visit and there is no evidence from the occupational therapist that the appellant has continued to use it or that she needs it. In her PIP2 the appellant said she has "No problems with the toilet itself" and she did not mention the frame at the consultation. She was observed to rise from a chair independently at the end of the consultation. The decision maker is satisfied that the appellant can manage her toilet needs unaided. Descriptor 5a applies.

The appellant's breathlessness when walking along the hall to the bathroom is not relevant to this activity, which concerns getting on and off the toilet and cleaning oneself afterwards.

## Section 7: Activities not in dispute

**Activity 4 (washing and bathing), descriptor b, 2 points.** The appellant uses a shower seat for every shower. This is supported by her PIP2 and by the occupational therapist. Not disputed.

**Activity 6 (dressing and undressing), descriptor b, 2 points.** On mandatory reconsideration the decision maker accepted the occupational therapist's evidence that the appellant uses a long-handled shoe horn daily to put on her shoes. Not disputed.

**Activities 2, 3, 7, 8, 9, 10 and 11.** There is no evidence of any difficulty with these activities. The appellant reports no problems eating and drinking, communicating, or mixing with other people; she reads with reading glasses only; she manages her own bills and shopping; and she knows her area and goes out alone. Her inhalers and blister pack do not involve supervision, prompting or assistance. Her limitation on journeys is physical and has been considered under activity 12. The representative accepts these descriptors. The decision maker submits that descriptor a applies in each case.

## Section 8: Conclusion

The decision maker has considered all the evidence. The appellant scores 4 points for daily living activities (activities 4b and 6b) and 4 points for mobility activities (activity 12b). She does not satisfy the conditions of entitlement to either component of PIP.

The tribunal is respectfully asked to confirm the decision of 22 July 2026 as revised on 9 September 2026.

If the tribunal is minded to award either component, the decision maker asks it to consider the appropriate length of award, noting the health professional's advice that some improvement is possible with pulmonary rehabilitation and suggested review in 2 years.
