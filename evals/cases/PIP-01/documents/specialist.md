Dear Adviser,

Thank you for your letter. I have known this lady since her diagnosis and see her every six months, with telephone reviews in between. Her MRI in May 2026 was stable and she has had no relapse since March 2024. Her disease-modifying treatment is dimethyl fumarate, which she manages herself.

Her principal problem is MS-related fatigue. This is not ordinary tiredness; on a bad day she describes being "wiped out" from waking and it does not improve with rest. Her fatigue does not follow a predictable weekly pattern, which is typical of MS fatigue. Heat and exertion the day before tend to make it worse.

She also has intermittent numbness and reduced fine sensation in both hands, worse in the right. On those days she reports dropping things and difficulty with buttons, zips and handling hot pans. Grip strength was reduced on the right at her last clinic visit, but I did not formally measure it.

Functional observations

On bad fatigue days she tells me she does very little beyond getting washed and dressed, and that her son does most of the meal preparation. She sits to shower and uses the shower stool supplied by OT in 2025, as she cannot stand for the length of a shower on any day.

On a good day she can walk around 200 to 300 metres at a steady pace. On bad days she reports needing to stop after about 50 metres because her legs feel heavy. I have not observed her walking outdoors. She does not use a walking aid.

Cognitively she reports slowed thinking on bad days but no concerns have been raised about memory or decision-making.

I would add that she tends to underplay her difficulties; she has often told me she is "fine" before describing a week in which she has not left the house.
