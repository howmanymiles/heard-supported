50s, lives with wife (works part-time, school hours). Former warehouse operative 14 yrs (picking, forklift). Dismissed on capability grounds Jan 2025. On UC since; currently fit notes, LCW not yet decided. UC50 completed by client alone, no help. Brought GP letter 02/09 and physio report 09/09.

Interview room: client stood up at approx 25 min, said needed to "stretch it out", stood by window approx 5 min then sat again. Shifted in chair repeatedly. Sat on edge of seat with one leg out straight.

**Mobilising**
Says on UC50 "about 100 yards". Asked to describe. Walks to corner shop (says about 2 min each way, did not know distance) most days. Has to stop at the bus shelter halfway on the way back "nearly every time". Leans on wall until leg eases, 1-2 min.
Good days / bad days: says "maybe three bad days a week" when he does not go out at all except to the garden. On bad days says he struggles to get from the house to the car. Has not kept a diary. Advised to keep a pain/walking diary for 4 weeks.
Has not been given a stick. Does not use one, says "I'm not using a stick at my age". Not discussed further.
Physio 6MWT: 240 m, stopped at 170 m. Client says that was "a good day, I'd taken extra tablets before".
Stairs: fine with rail, slow.

**Standing / sitting**
UC50 says sit 20 min. Physio records client reported "up to an hour in a supportive chair". Asked about this: client says he meant he can stay in a chair for an hour if he can "wriggle about" and put his feet up, not upright at a desk. At home sits in recliner. Standing 10-15 min, consistent across docs.
Can't stand to wash dishes, wife does them. Sits on a stool to shave.

**Upper body**
No issues reaching. Can lift kettle, light shopping. Bending to floor painful, uses reacher. No issue with hands.

**Mental health**
GAD diagnosed 2025. On sertraline 100 mg. Waiting list for CBT, told 6-9 months. No secondary care MH involvement. No history of self-harm, says no thoughts of harming himself (asked as routine).
Worry most of the day, poor sleep (pain and worry).
Change: wife (2nd half) gives examples: physio cancelled on the morning in July, he went back to bed and "didn't speak all day". Bus not turning up, comes home rather than wait for the next one, misses appointments. When his brother changed a family visit to a different day with a week's notice he "went on about it for days" but did go. Wife says this happens "most weeks, something sets him off".
Planned change: managed with notice, per client and wife, although upset.

**Social**
Client says fine with family and old workmates. Strangers: says he "gets on with it" but avoids. Did not want to go to the pain management group: "a room full of folk I don't know talking about it". Wife says he will speak to shop staff, the GP, the jobcentre work coach. Not clear he is unable to engage with unfamiliar people most of the time. Did talk to me without obvious difficulty, though tense at start.
Going out: goes alone to familiar places. Went to physio (new place) on his own by bus first time. Says he prefers wife to drive him but she works.

**Other**
Starting/finishing tasks: some mornings doesn't get up till late after bad night. Wife says he starts jobs in the garden and leaves them because of pain, not because he forgets. Physical, not cognitive (adviser view).
No continence, consciousness, communication issues.
Driving: still drives short distances, 10-15 min.

**Actions**
1. Client to keep walking/pain diary 4 weeks.
2. Request GP comment on walking distance (GP letter says not formally assessed).
3. Ask physio to clarify sitting tolerance in a work setting.
4. Draft WCA submission: combined physical and mental points.
